### Abstract
Divergent behaviors can facilitate species radiations, potentially contributing to both pre- and postzygotic barriers. Although this often involves male and female behaviors, it is unclear how effectively individual behaviors contribute to reproductive isolation, particularly at early stages of speciation. Using no-choice behavioral assays, we examine this question using three species of Chorthippus grasshoppers that diverged recently in the presence of gene flow. Our results show that all tested courtship behaviors depend on whether individuals are paired with con- or heterospecific individuals, culminating in complete prezygotic isolation. Behavioral isolation is initiated by male courtship singing, with a strong preference for conspecific females, showing a substantial contribution of male preference to reproductive isolation. All interspecific crosses showed the same components of behavioral isolation and similar strength regardless of genetic divergence, suggesting that these individual components can evolve rapidly and effectively facilitate speciation. Our study supports the role of male preference in assortative mating and highlights the importance of coupling individual behavioral barriers to facilitate rapid radiations.
Keywords: biguttulus group, sexual selection, behavioral isolation
